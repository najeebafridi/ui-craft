Chapter 3: The Salt Road

Novel · Edited 4 minutes ago · 0 words

Marta woke before the others, as she always did, to the sound of the tide pulling gravel back across the harbour wall. The lamp had burned down to a thumb of grey wax. Somewhere below, a gull argued with the morning and lost.

She dressed in the dark. Wool stockings, the heavy skirt her mother had hemmed twice, her father's coat with its pockets still smelling faintly of tar. In the left pocket she found the letter again, folded to the size of a playing card, and she did not read it, because she knew it by heart.

The road to Aldmouth is eleven miles, and the salt carts will not wait for anyone.

Eleven miles. She had walked farther for worse reasons. At the door she paused, listening to Tomas breathe in the loft, slow and even, a boy who still believed the world kept its promises. She left the bread on the table where he would find it and pulled the latch closed behind her with both hands, the way you close a door on a sleeping child.

Outside, the fog had settled over the marsh like a held breath. The path was only a darker idea of the ground. She followed it by memory and by the cold seeping through the soles of her boots, and when the first cart rattled out of the murk ahead of her, she was not surprised to see who was driving it.

“You came,” Henrik said, without turning his head.

“You said you wouldn’t wait.”

“I said a great many things.” He held out a hand, and after a moment she took it and climbed up beside him among the sacks, each one stamped with the grey crest of the Aldmouth works. The horse leaned into the harness. Behind them the village went on sleeping, and ahead the road began, white as a seam, running out toward the sea.
